## The product

Taconomics is a decentralized app (DApp): a launchpad where Latino artists can make their digital art known. In practice, it's an NFT gallery where every piece is related to tacos, and $TACO is the platform's cryptocurrency.

## Requirements

I started by listing the product's functional and non-functional requirements with the founder. Collectors needed to:

- Explore and buy digital art.
- See the pieces they bought.
- Buy $TACO.
- Stake $Salsa, a second currency, to earn Lemons, the main currency for buying art.
- Learn about the project and its smart contracts.
- Contact the team to apply as an artist.

Artists needed to:

- Create collections.
- Post their art.
- Claim their earnings from NFTs sold.

## Who it's for

Three user profiles came out of the requirements:

- **Collectors**: people who buy digital art.
- **Artists**: people who publish and sell their art.
- **Administrators**: people who edit the site's content.

All three connect an Ethereum wallet through Metamask. Visitors without a wallet can only browse.

## Key decisions

### A slider for the two main actions

I first proposed a simple hero: heading, image and a call to action. The founder suggested a slider with the platform's main actions instead, and it was the better idea. It has two slides: one for buying art, one for selling it, each with custom illustrations.

![The home slider for buying and selling art](/case-studies/taconomics/06-slider.webp)

### Cards that reveal the artist

On hover, an art card shows a description of the artist and a link to their full collection.

![An art card and its hover state](/case-studies/taconomics/07-card.webp)

### Buy $TACO and stake $Salsa on one page

Collectors can't buy art without $TACO and $Salsa, so the founder and I put both on the same page, with a switch between them.

- **Buy $TACO** is a standard swap, like most crypto sites.
- **Stake $Salsa** is more complex. Salsa can be made two ways: multi-asset, with $TACO and WETH together, or single-asset, with either one.

A select chooses between multi-asset and single-asset, so the switch isn't overloaded. In single-asset mode, the choice between $TACO and WETH uses option buttons designed not to look like option buttons: the selected token sits in a card.

![Buy $TACO and make $Salsa, with the multi-asset and single-asset modes](/case-studies/taconomics/08-buy-stake.webp)
![Make $Salsa in single-asset mode, final design](/case-studies/taconomics/27-make-salsa.webp)

## Brand

The founder wanted a logo and some branding. I suggested a 3D style; he wanted something flat, so we went flat.

### Typography

I gave the founder five font options that fit something flat and not too serious. The designs use Nunito, with Montserrat in a few UI elements.

![Font options](/case-studies/taconomics/09-fonts.webp)

### Logo

A taco with a smile. The "i" is shaped like the salsa bottles at Mexican taco stands.
