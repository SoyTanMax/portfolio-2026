# Pide Directo Web Menu

### Cutting the friction between the menu and the first order. Designed and built in React.

<Summary
  role="Led the team that redesigned and rebuilt the web menu in React. Other people on the team worked on the components."
  outcome="A mobile-first ordering flow for a product where almost 90% of orders came from phones, with live order status after ordering."
  decision="Sign-up inside checkout with only a phone number, verified by SMS, instead of email + phone + email confirmation."

## The problem

Pide Directo is a web menu where restaurant customers order directly. Almost 90% of customers ordered from their phones, but the menu wasn't built for mobile.

The goal was more completed orders. That meant a better experience for two groups: the customers ordering, and the restaurants selling.

## The order flow as a funnel

We treated the flow from menu to confirmed order as one funnel. At every step, the question was the same: what could make someone leave here?

- **Menu**: find something to order fast, on a small screen.
- **Product**: choose options without losing the way back to the menu.
- **Cart and sign-up**: the step with the most friction, and the one we changed the most.
- **Order details and confirmation**: check everything before placing the order.
- **After ordering**: know where the order is.

## Key decisions

### Sign-up happens inside checkout

Customers sign up when they're about to order, not before. By then they already want something, so the form is a step toward their food, not a gate in front of the menu.

### Phone number only, verified by SMS

Before, signing up required both an email and a phone number, plus email confirmation. Now it only takes a phone number, verified with a code sent by SMS. The email is no longer required.

This is an activation decision. Confirming an email means leaving the app, finding the message and coming back, right when the customer is trying to order. An SMS code arrives on the phone most customers were already ordering from. Every extra step there is a chance to lose the order.

### Live order status

The redesign added order states, so customers know exactly where their order is after placing it.

### Mobile-first, then responsive

With almost 90% of orders on phones, every screen was designed for mobile first. Desktop views came from the mobile design, not the other way around. Reusable components kept both consistent.

## Building it

We rebuilt the whole frontend in React. The old one wasn't made for mobile, so this wasn't a reskin.

Much of the backend already existed as functions in the restaurant's POS system. The new frontend was built on top of them.

We tested on different devices and shipped quick fixes after launch.

## What changed

The app became visibly more functional and easier to use. Customers could follow their order's status from start to finish.

Sales and checkout conversion weren't measured, so there are no numbers here.

## What I'd measure

- **Funnel conversion**: the share of people who move from each step to the next, from opening the menu to a confirmed order. Where the biggest drop is.
- **Sign-up**: how many people who start signing up finish, and the time from sign-up to first order. Compared before and after switching to phone-only sign-up.
- **Mobile vs. desktop**: the same funnel, split by device.
- **SMS verification**: how many codes are sent but never entered, and how often customers ask for a new one. That's where the new sign-up can still lose people.

## What I'd do next

- Test the checkout with real customers.
- Instrument the funnel above before the next change, to get a before and after.
